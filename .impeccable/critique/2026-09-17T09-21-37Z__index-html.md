## Overall Impression

This round confirms the app is holding its design line well under the DESIGN.md spec written after the last critique: the unset-par treatment, the completion gate, the armed two-tap confirm pattern, all render and behave exactly as documented. The one real problem is a functional bug, not a design one: editing Stock silently discards a manually-typed To Order value, which directly breaks the "auto-calculates but is manually overridable" promise in PRODUCT.md. That's worse than a visual nit, it can cause real under- or over-ordering with zero warning to the person doing it.

## What's Working

1. The unset-par treatment (em-dash, dashed border, italic, faded color) renders exactly as DESIGN.md specifies and reads clearly at a glance.
2. The category-complete flow (green fill, auto-collapse, auto-scroll to next section) removes the need to hunt for what's next.
3. The destructive-action pattern (armed icon + inline toast + 3s window) reads calmer than a native browser dialog and was implemented faithfully to the DESIGN.md spec.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Manual To-Order overrides get silently discarded**
Why it matters: `setStock()` (app.js:227-235) unconditionally calls `setOrder(suggested)` every time Stock changes, even if the user had just typed a different To Order value moments before. This breaks PRODUCT.md's explicit "manually overridable" contract and can silently cause real under/over-ordering.
Fix: track whether the current To Order value still equals the last auto-suggested one; only re-suggest when it does, leave it alone once the user has diverged from the suggestion.

**[P1] Disabled Send/Review buttons don't read as disabled**
Why it matters: `.btn:disabled { opacity: 0.4 }` (styles.css:407) barely dims the accent color against the near-black background. Paired directly under a loud amber warning box, it creates a "the warning says no but the button looks yes" mismatch right when a time-pressured user is deciding whether they're done.
Fix: desaturate disabled buttons to a neutral gray instead of a dimmed accent, consistent with the system's own rule that the accent color means "actionable" - a disabled button isn't.

**[P1] Zero bulk actions on the largest sheet**
Why it matters: Makro is 11 categories and 180 items with no way to filter to "only items I've touched" or skip a category with nothing needed. This is the dominant driver of cognitive load on the sheet that matters most, and directly hurts the "faster than the spreadsheet" success metric from PRODUCT.md.
Fix: even a lightweight toggle to show only items with a quantity entered, or a one-tap "nothing needed here" per category, would cut scroll and decision volume substantially.

**[P2] Native browser focus ring breaks the palette**
Why it matters: DESIGN.md already flags this as "a known gap, not an intentional choice." The default blue focus outline is the single most visually jarring moment in the app, precisely because blue appears nowhere else in the system.
Fix: add a `:focus-visible` treatment using Charred Terracotta or Warm Parchment.

**[P2] Par field is visibly shorter than its row-mates**
Why it matters: `.par-value` (styles.css:230) has no explicit height while the Stock input and stepper beside it are both 44px, so the three-field row is subtly misaligned on every item, undercutting the "one input family" language in DESIGN.md.
Fix: give `.par-value` `min-height: 44px` to match.

## Persona Red Flags

**The Stocktaker (mid-shift, editing stock counts as they physically count items)**: types a To Order value they know is correct from experience, then goes back to fix a stock-count typo, and their override vanishes without any indication it happened. They won't necessarily notice until the wrong quantity ships.

**The Time-Pressured Closer (finishing the Makro sheet at the end of a long list)**: sees a bright orange Send Order button that looks tappable, taps it, nothing happens because a category upstream was never marked complete, and has to scroll back up to find out why. The disabled state doesn't visually rule itself out.
