## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated? No.** Both assessments agree.

**LLM assessment**: near-black tinted background, single warm accent, system font stack, flat 1px-bordered cards, no gradients, no glassmorphism, no glow shadows, no hero-metric tiles, no side-stripe borders, no modal-as-default. Blunt copy ("This cannot be undone") instead of softened SaaS voice. Would pass as a bespoke internal tool.

**Deterministic scan** (`npx impeccable detect index.html`): 3 findings, all the same root cause - **low-contrast text**, white (`#ffffff`) on the accent orange (`#e0673f`) used for primary buttons. Measured 3.4:1, needs 4.5:1 for body text. This hits every `.btn` (Review Order, Send Order, Mark Category Complete states, branch/supplier buttons) - it's the single most-used interactive element in the app failing WCAG AA, which is a direct miss against PRODUCT.md's own accessibility line ("sufficient contrast on the dark theme"). No false positives to note; this is a real, fixable contrast issue (darken the accent slightly or use a near-black label instead of pure white).

## Overall Impression

The restraint is real and earned: nothing here reads as generated, and the domain modeling (par/stock/to-order, MOQ gating, the burger-patty combo calculator) shows actual thought about the workflow, not templated CRUD. The gap is between what the UI *promises* and what the code *enforces*. Two things staff would reasonably assume are safeguards, that the to-order field auto-calculates from a real par level, and that "Mark Category Complete" gates sending, are both cosmetic right now: every par in data.js is 0, and completion state is never checked before the email goes out. That's the single biggest opportunity: close the gap between the UI's promises and its guarantees before polishing anything visual.

## What's Working

1. **Autosave/resume architecture** - per branch+supplier draft in localStorage, with auto-resume of the last branch/supplier on reload, genuinely serves the "interruptible, one-handed, put the phone down mid-stocktake" persona from PRODUCT.md.
2. **Restraint** - resists every generic-SaaS impulse (no dashboard cards, no gradient CTAs, no onboarding modal), staying true to "numbers over decoration."
3. **Real domain logic** - MOQ enforcement and the burger-patty yield calculator (kg ordered -> patty count) are genuinely useful, non-generic features that show the app was built around the actual job, not a generic form builder.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] To-order auto-calculation is inert in production data**
Why it matters: every one of the 205 `par` values in data.js is 0, so the headline feature ("to-order auto-calculates from a par level, but is manually overridable") never actually fires - staff must manually type every quantity, which is *slower* than the spreadsheet this app replaced and defeats the stated "fewer ordering errors, faster than the spreadsheet" success metric.
Fix: populate real par values (you mentioned uploading these yourself), and render an unset par distinctly from a real "stock to zero" par (e.g. "—" vs "0") so staff can tell whether auto-suggest actually ran.
Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify` for the unset-vs-zero copy/display fix; the data population itself is a data.js edit, not a design task.

**[P0] "Mark Category Complete" isn't enforced before sending**
Why it matters: `sendOrder()` never checks `state.completed`. An order can be emailed with 0 of 11 categories verified and lands on the exact same reassuring green confirm screen as a fully-checked one. This is the biggest miss against the app's own stated purpose (reducing ordering errors) - the one verification step the UI implies is mandatory is, in the code, just a personal checklist.
Fix: either block Send until all categories are marked (matches what the UI currently implies), or explicitly downgrade the copy/warning so staff know it's optional and the incomplete-category warning should persist onto the confirm screen instead of vanishing.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden` (this is an edge-case/error-prevention gap, not a visual one).

**[P1] Highest-frequency controls are under the product's own 44px tap-target floor**
Why it matters: PRODUCT.md sets 44px as the accessibility floor for a "one-handed, carrying stock" user, but the stepper +/- buttons are 30x34px and the topbar icon buttons are 36x36px. The stepper is tapped per item, potentially hundreds of times per order - it's the single most-repeated interaction in the app and it's undersized for its own stated persona.
Fix: enlarge hit areas to >=44x44px (padding can keep the visual glyph compact); this pairs naturally with the contrast fix below since both touch `.stepper button` and `.icon-btn`.
Suggested command: `/impeccable adapt` or `/impeccable audit` to sweep for other sub-44px targets while in there.

**[P1] Primary buttons fail WCAG AA contrast**
Why it matters: white text on the `#e0673f` accent measures 3.4:1 against a 4.5:1 requirement, on every `.btn` in the app (Review Order, Send Order, branch/supplier picks) - the buttons staff rely on most to move the task forward.
Fix: darken the accent by a few percent lightness, or switch button label color to a near-black tone instead of pure white, then re-run `npx impeccable detect index.html` to confirm 0 findings.
Suggested command: `/impeccable audit` (accessibility pass) or a direct fix now, given the fix is a one-line CSS change.

**[P2] Native `confirm()` dialogs contradict the app's own "state is the feedback" principle**
Why it matters: Clear All and Change Supplier both use the browser's native `confirm()`, which is unstyled, breaks the dark theme, and directly contradicts PRODUCT.md's design principle ("state is the feedback... replace toasts and modals"). It's compounded by the trash-can and switch-supplier icon buttons sitting adjacent at identical size, differing only by a small glyph - a mis-tap risk under time pressure, right before an unstyled system dialog fires.
Fix: replace with an in-page confirm state (e.g. a second-tap-to-confirm or an inline banner), and visually differentiate the two icon buttons.
Suggested command: `/impeccable polish` once P0/P1s are addressed.

## Persona Red Flags

**The Stocktaker (primary persona - staff mid-shift, one hand full of stock, phone in the other)**: Taps the +/- stepper repeatedly on a sub-44px target while not looking closely at the screen; on the 48-item Produce category, the `has-qty` highlight is only an 8%-opacity tint, easy to miss when scanning which rows already have a quantity. Marks a category complete, watches it auto-scroll to the next one so fast the green "completed" confirmation barely registers before it's gone. At the end, sends the order without noticing (or caring, since nothing enforces it) that 3 categories were never marked complete - the app doesn't tell them.

**The New Hire (untrained on this specific tool, handed a phone and told "just do the order")**: Sees "Par 0 / Stock [blank] / To Order 0" on every single item and has no way to know whether that's real ("we truly want zero of this") or the app simply not knowing pars yet - there's no visual distinction and no help text. Types every quantity in manually, same as they would have on paper, gaining none of the promised speed benefit.

## Minor Observations

- Default blue browser focus ring appears on the Stock input, clashing with the warm dark palette; no `:focus-visible` style defined.
- The auto-scroll-to-next-category on completion moves the newly-green card off-screen almost immediately, undercutting its own positive reinforcement.
- Confirm screen copy asserts the mail app "has opened" unconditionally, regardless of whether a mail client is actually configured on the device.

## Questions to Consider

- If every par value ships as 0, has par-driven auto-order ever actually functioned for staff, or has "auto-calculates from a par level" been aspirational copy since launch?
- If an order can be sent with zero categories marked complete, what is "Mark Category Complete" actually for: a mandatory gate (as the UI implies) or a personal to-do (as the code enforces)? Right now it's neither, consistently.
- Given the explicit one-handed, storage-room persona, why are the two highest-frequency controls (stepper, icon buttons) sized below the floor the product spec itself sets?
